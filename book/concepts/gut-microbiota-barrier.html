Gut Microbiota & Intestinal Barrier

The "microbiota–barrier–LPS" axis: Gram-negative dysbiosis raises lipopolysaccharide (LPS) translocation → TLR4 activation → TNF-α/IL-6 release → chronic low-grade inflammation → insulin resistance and hyperglycemia.

  • Obese mice (ob/ob, db/db) show downregulated tight-junction proteins (Occludin, ZO-1) and elevated portal-vein LPS ("portal endotoxemia") (Brun et al.).
  • Cani et al.: high-fat diet damages the intestinal barrier; broad-spectrum antibiotics reversed permeability, plasma LPS, and weight gain — proof that the microbiota drives the inflammatory cascade.
  • IF's effect: IF enriches beneficial bacteria (Lactobacillus, Bifidobacterium, Akkermansia muciniphila) and SCFA production; SCFAs upregulate Occludin/ZO-1 and MUC-2, limiting LPS translocation.
  • Human signal: Guo et al. — metabolic-syndrome patients on 5:2 for 8 weeks showed ↑ plasma SCFAs and ↓ LPS, i.e. enhanced intestinal barrier function.
  • GLP-1 link: SCFAs stimulate GLP-1 secretion from enteroendocrine L-cells via GPR41/43, directly improving insulin sensitivity and glycemic control (Figure 3 in the paper).

Caveats: most mechanistic detail is from rodent models; no head-to-head IF vs. CER mechanistic study exists.
